## Level 0

### Example 1

**Prompt:**

Modal equivalence. Consider degrees of modality: 'must' and 'need to' and 'is required to' express necessity; 'may' and 'can' express permissibility. Putting 'not' above a modal (e.g. 'does not need to') negates the modal force; putting 'not' below it (e.g. 'may not') negates the proposition the modal scopes over. Two phrasings are equivalent when they agree on force (necessity vs permission) and on the polarity of the proposition. 
Sentence A: the delegate cannot enter the data.
Sentence B: the delegate is not permitted to enter the data.
Write the canonical normalized form of Sentence A's reading, choosing exactly one of: 'must', 'must not', 'may', 'may not'. Answer with that form only.


**Answer:**

may not
