## Level 0

### Example 1

Prompt:

Let A, B, C, D denote matrices of size 2×2 over the complex numbers; each letter may be replaced by any 2×2 matrix. A commutator [X,Y] is the alternating sum X·Y − Y·X, and tr(Z) is the matrix trace. Multiplication is noncommutative matrix multiplication. The expression E below is a combination of trace terms with the shown integer coefficients. E is a universal identity if its value is zero for every replacement of the letters by 2×2 matrices. For example, tr(A·B) − tr(B·A) is a universal identity, while tr(A·A) is not. State whether E is a universal identity, answering with the single word identity or nonidentity.

E = -1·tr([B,A]·A·[B,A]) + 1·tr(A·[B,A]·[B,A])

Determine whether E vanishes for every 2×2 substitution.

Answer: identity

E = -1·tr([C,D]·D·[C,A]·A·[C,A]) + 1·tr(A·[C,A]·[C,D]·D·[C,A]) + 3·tr(B·A·[B,A]·[B,A]) − 2·tr(A·[A,A]·[B,B]·[B,D]·[B,C]) + 2·tr([B,D]·[B,C]·A·[A,A]·[B,B]) − 3·tr([B,A]·B·A·[B,A])
