## Level 0
### Example 1
Prompt:

A gambler runs a sequential hypothesis test over 2 days. Each day the world sits in one of two regimes, A or B, following a null transition grid (from the day's regime to the next): A->A = 2/3, A->B = 1/3, B->A = 1/2, B->B = 1/2. The bettor's wealth is updated at the end of each day depending on that day's ending regime: in regime A wealth is multiplied by 2 then a fixed rebate of -2 is added; in regime B wealth is multiplied by 3 then a fixed rebate of -1 is added. The starting regime is A with probability 3/4 and B with probability 1/4. The bettor stakes an amount S up front, so wealth starts at S. The strategy is a valid test only if, under the null law, expected wealth never increases from one day to the next (the wealth process is a supermartingale): E[W_t] <= E[W_{t-1}] at every day t = 1..2, where the expectation marginalizes over all regime paths. Find the maximal initial stake S for which the strategy is valid. Give S as a reduced fraction a/b, or as a whole integer if the denominator is 1 (for example, an answer could look like 7/4 or 3).

Answer: 8/7
